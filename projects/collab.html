Undergraduate Engineering Research

Simulation-driven design, structural analysis, and optimization for advanced engineering systems

Overview

As an undergraduate researcher, I worked on simulation-driven mechanical design problems involving structural optimization, material selection, and performance validation under real-world loading conditions. My work emphasized using engineering analysis tools to inform design decisions early in the development cycle.

Role & Responsibilities

  • Conducted finite element analysis (FEA) to evaluate stress, deformation, and failure modes
  • Performed topology optimization to reduce mass while preserving structural stiffness
  • Interpreted simulation results to guide CAD iteration and geometry refinement
  • Collaborated with faculty and team members to align design choices with research objectives
  • Documented results and design rationale for internal review and presentation

Tools & Methods

  • ANSYS Mechanical (Static Structural, Topology Optimization)
  • CAD-based parametric modeling
  • Engineering materials analysis
  • Simulation validation and result interpretation

Design Impact

Simulation results directly influenced geometry changes, material selection, and reinforcement strategies. By identifying stress concentrations and inefficient load paths early, the design achieved improved performance without unnecessary mass or overengineering.

Key Takeaways

  • Engineering intuition improves dramatically when paired with validated simulation
  • Topology optimization is a powerful tool, but must be interpreted with manufacturability in mind
  • Clear visualization of results is essential for communicating technical decisions
  • Analysis-driven design reduces iteration time and improves confidence in final hardware
