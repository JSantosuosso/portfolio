Cephalobot — Soft Robotic Squid

Robotics · Soft Actuation · Pneumatics · Manufacturing

Project Summary

Work in progress! Part of Soft Robotics course, studying swim mechanics and biomimicry to design a 6" long squid soft robot for a race. Working as part of a 4-person team to design, 3D print molds, cast silicone body and bladder, and manufacture check valves. Utilized bistable geometry to design silicone air bladder, control inflation with Arduino to achieve jet propulsion. Robot is designed to compete in a race to cross a 45"x19"x15" water tank completely submerged.

Key Subsystems

1. Pneumatic Bladder Design

Designed the internal bladder geometry to maximize stroke volume while keeping wall thickness uniform for reliable molding. The bladder's air supply is controlled by an external regulator, which is controlled by an Arduino. The controlled inflation and deflation of the bladder will be used to intake water and then expel it. The vacuum created by the expulsion of water and the check valves will "pull" in water from the front, which can then be used to repeat the cycle.

2. Check Valve Fabrication

Using thin acetate, water will only be allowed to flow into the chamber through the top intake, and then only allowed to leave through the bottom outtake, optimizing for forward propulsion.

3. Nose

The nose is designed for two concepts. The curvature of it is meant to lower drag and then encourage vortices of water to enter the intake. The fins aid in stabilization. The nose is also designed for variable weight distribution. Magnets can be placed in the front to control the orientation of the robot, helping to direct propulsion.

Manufacturing & Testing

Used 3D printing for molds, silicone casting for bladders, and a Cricut for valve films. Currently finishing assembly, and working with the regulator to control inflation of the bladder .

Outcome

The final Cephalobot is almost ready, as the race is coming soon in early December (results will be posted here). The bladder performed well with visible bistability when subject to the inhalation and exhalation of a team member. A TPU shell may replace the silicone one for a lighter robot.
